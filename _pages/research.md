<div class="activities research">
  <section class="activity-section">
    <h2 class="category">What I work on</h2>
    <p>
      My research sits where natural language processing meets society. I study how language technologies detect, judge and
      sometimes reproduce harmful content online, and how to make them fairer, more transparent and more efficient.
    </p>
    <ul class="activity-list">
      <li class="activity-item">
        <div class="activity-body">
          <span class="activity-title">Hate speech detection with large language models</span>
          <span class="activity-note">
            How LLMs perceive, assess and react to hate speech, and how far we can rely on them as annotators and evaluators.
          </span>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="activity-item">
        <div class="activity-body">
          <span class="activity-title">Bias and perspectivism</span>
          <span class="activity-note">
            Geographic and demographic bias in hate speech detection. Hate speech is subjective, so I study how models handle the
            differing judgements of the groups most affected instead of collapsing them into a single label.
          </span>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="activity-item">
        <div class="activity-body">
          <span class="activity-title">Efficient and explainable models</span>
          <span class="activity-note">
            Smaller models that keep the accuracy of large ones and explain their decisions, through distillation and reasoning.
          </span>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="activity-item">
        <div class="activity-body">
          <span class="activity-title">Datasets for multilingual and low-resource settings</span>
          <span class="activity-note">
            Meta-collections, benchmarks and new datasets that make hate speech research possible beyond English.
          </span>
        </div>
      </li>
    </ul>
  </section>
